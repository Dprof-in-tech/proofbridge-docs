title: "Makers and Bridgers: understanding ProofBridge roles"

---

Every cross-chain transfer involves two parties: a **Maker** who provides
liquidity on one chain, and a **Bridger** who moves assets from the other.
Neither needs to trust the other: settlement needs **both parties' BLS
co-signatures** and an on-chain zero-knowledge proof of each deposit.

## The two roles

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Maker" icon="droplet">
    A liquidity provider who posts an ad, locks liquidity for each order,
    and receives the Bridger's tokens at settlement, plus a fee. A Maker
    can serve trades by hand or let an [agent](/v3/concepts/agents) lock
    orders within limits they set.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Bridger" icon="bridge">
    A user moving assets across chains. Bridgers deposit against a
    matching ad and receive the Maker's tokens on the other chain.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Both roles share one prerequisite: the trading wallet needs a registered
[settlement key](/v3/concepts/settlement-keys), a one-time step, because
every trade settles only with both parties' signatures.

## How Makers work

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create and fund an ad">
    Post an ad for a route and deposit liquidity into the `AdManager`
    contract. The ad names your settlement signer: the wallet whose key
    co-signs your side of each trade.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lock for an order">
    When a Bridger funds an order against your ad, lock the matching
    liquidity. This is your deposit into the trade. An agent can do this
    for you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Co-sign">
    Once both deposits confirm, sign the settlement with your settlement
    key, in the app, with no transaction.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receive the Bridger's tokens">
    The relayer combines the signatures, proves both deposits and submits
    the payouts. You receive the Bridger's full deposit, and the LP fee
    stays in your ad.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How Bridgers work

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick a route and deposit">
    Choose an ad with enough liquidity, enter the amount and fund the
    order. Your tokens go into the `OrderPortal` escrow. Native ETH and
    XLM are wrapped automatically.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Co-sign">
    When both deposits confirm, one click signs the settlement with your
    settlement key. The trade can't settle without it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receive your tokens">
    The same process settles both chains. You receive the amount less the
    [fee](/v3/concepts/fees) at your receive address.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  No sign-up is required. Your connected wallet is your identity, and your
  settlement key derives from a wallet signature.
</Note>

## Comparing the two roles

| | Maker | Bridger |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary action** | Posts liquidity ads | Opens orders against ads |
| **What you lock** | Ad-chain tokens | Order-chain tokens |
| **What you receive** | The Bridger's full deposit, plus the LP fee kept in the ad | The amount less the fee |
| **Co-signature** | Required (by hand, or the settlement signer you named) | Required, one click |
| **If the trade doesn't settle** | Your lock returns to the ad | Your deposit is refunded |
| **Can file a dispute** | Yes | Yes |
| **On-chain contract** | `AdManager` | `OrderPortal` |

## What protects both sides

Settlement needs three things, all checked on-chain: your signature, the
counterparty's signature (combined into one), and a zero-knowledge proof
of each deposit. The relayer can't forge either signature, can't invent a
deposit, and holds no funds. If a trade doesn't settle by its deadline,
the [cancel path](/v3/concepts/cancels-and-refunds) returns each side's
funds, and either side can [file a dispute](/v3/concepts/disputes).

A Maker can also **halt settlement** on their ads in one transaction,
for example if their settlement key may be compromised. While halted, no
co-signed payout goes through on that Maker's ads; trades end through the
cancel path instead.
