---
title: "Root anchors"
description: "How one chain learns the other chain's Merkle root when no co-signature exists"
---

A normal payout carries both chains' Merkle roots inside the parties'
[co-signature](/v3/concepts/co-signing), so the roots are vouched for by
the two traders. Some paths have no co-signature: a cancel, a forfeit,
recording that a trade settled. For those, each chain needs another way to
know the other chain's root. That's what the `RootAnchor` contract holds.

## What an anchor is

The **anchor service** reads each chain's latest finalized MMR root and
publishes it to the `RootAnchor` contract on the other chain. A published
root becomes usable only after the route's **anchor delay**: 5 minutes on
soak. The delay gives time to spot and revoke a wrong anchor before any
contract relies on it.

## What uses anchors

| Path | Proves | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refund after a cancel | the cancel leaf on the ad chain | an anchored ad-chain root |
| Paying the Maker after a bridger forfeit | the forfeit leaf on the ad chain | an anchored ad-chain root |
| Cancelling a never-locked order | the Bridger's deposit on the order chain | an anchored order-chain root |
| Recording that the other side settled | the settled leaf | an anchored root of the other chain |

Normal settlement never uses an anchor.

## If the anchor service is unavailable

Settlement carries on as normal. The paths in the table wait: if the
anchor service is unavailable, refunds on the order chain wait until it
returns, because the order chain has no clock of its own and only follows
the ad chain's proven outcome.

## Checking an anchor yourself

An anchored root is the root the source chain's own `MerkleManager`
reports. You can rebuild it from the chain's events with
[`proofbridge-mmr`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/proofbridge-mmr) and
compare.
