---
title: "Deposit proofs"
description: "How one chain verifies a deposit, or any other trade event, that happened on another"
---

The destination chain can't read the source chain. What carries the fact
"the counterparty really deposited" across is a **zero-knowledge proof**.

## The Merkle Mountain Range

Every deposit adds a leaf to that chain's on-chain
[Merkle Mountain Range (MMR)](/v3/concepts/merkle-mountain-range), an
append-only tree whose root commits to every event ever recorded there.
Each leaf binds the trade's order hash and a **leaf domain**: what kind of
event it records. A proof about one event can't be bent to serve another.

## One circuit, several events

A single circuit, the **events circuit**, proves that a leaf of a given
domain is in a given root. The contracts always set the domain
themselves; it's never something the caller picks.

| Domain | Event | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | the Maker's lock (on the ad chain) | the payout to the Maker on the order chain |
| 1 | the Bridger's deposit (on the order chain) | the payout to the Bridger on the ad chain |
| 2 | a cancel | the Bridger's refund on the order chain |
| 3 | a settled payout | recording that the other side settled |
| 4 | a key registration | registration by proof (not active yet) |
| 5 | a forfeit | paying the Maker after a "bridger forfeit" ruling |

A deposit proof (domains 0 and 1) also proves knowledge of that payout's
[half of the trade secret](/v3/concepts/trade-secrets), which fixes its
nullifier. The other domains carry no secret.

## The proof

At payout time, a proof is built showing the counterparty's deposit leaf
is in the *other* chain's MMR root, the same root both parties co-signed.
The contract verifies the proof on-chain, together with the nullifier
that makes each payout happen exactly once.

The cancel, forfeit and settled proofs are checked against a root
published by the [anchor service](/v3/concepts/root-anchors) instead,
because no co-signature exists on those paths.

## Speed

A deposit proof takes **seconds** to build, and a CI benchmark fails the
build if it regresses. Proofs are built per trade, per chain; you never
wait on them.

## Verification on both chains

- **Ethereum** verifies with a generated UltraHonk verifier contract.
- **Soroban** verifies with a reusable verifier that takes the
  verification key as data.

A full payout on Sepolia, with the BLS co-signature check and the proof,
costs roughly **2.4M gas**, guarded by a CI gas ceiling.

Together with the combined BLS signature check, this is the whole
authority of a settlement: *both traders signed, and both deposits
provably exist.*
