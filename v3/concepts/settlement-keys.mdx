---
title: "Settlement keys"
description: "The per-wallet BLS key that co-signs every trade: how it's derived, registered, retired and replaced"
---

Every trading wallet holds a **BLS12-381 settlement key**. At payout, the
contracts look up the registered keys for the trade's two parties and
check the combined signature against them. The keys are how each chain
knows *both traders* approved the settlement. A settlement key signs
settlement messages only; it never controls your funds.

## Derived, not stored

The key is derived from a wallet signature over a fixed message:

```
ProofBridge BLS key derivation v1 — do not sign on other dapps | key #0000
```

Sign the same message with the same wallet and you get the same key, on
any device. There is no seed phrase to back up, and the server never sees
the key. EVM wallets sign the message as a personal message; Stellar
wallets sign it with SEP-53. The app keeps an encrypted copy in your
browser, so routine co-signs need only one wallet tap. The registry on
each chain stays the source of truth.

The `key #NNNN` counter is the **key index**. A replacement key uses the
next index, so the message changes, the signature changes and the key
changes.

## Registration

Registration writes your key to the `BLSKeyRegistry` on **both** chains,
because a trade settles on both. It:

- carries a **proof of possession**: a BLS signature showing you hold the
  key you're registering, so nobody can register a key they don't hold
- is authorized by **one wallet signature** that covers both registries
  (EIP-712 for an EVM wallet, SEP-53 for a Stellar wallet)
- is submitted by the relayer, which pays the gas. You can submit the same
  registration yourself instead.

To register on both chains, sign with a regular wallet: an EVM wallet that
holds its own private key, or a Stellar account signing with its own key.
Smart-contract wallets (a Safe, a 4337 wallet, a Stellar passkey or
contract account) can't register on both chains yet.

## Per wallet, not per user

The contracts check the keys of the trade's two deposit wallets. They have
no notion of a "user" with linked wallets. If you make markets from a
Stellar wallet and bridge from an EVM wallet, each registers its own key.

## Several keys at once

An account can have up to **five** live keys at a time, each with an
optional expiry. That lets a new key go live before the old one ends, and
lets an agent's key sit alongside your own.

When an order is locked, the Maker's co-signing key for that trade is
**pinned**. If that key ends before the trade settles, both parties are
asked to sign again with a live key.

## Naming a settlement signer

Each ad names the wallet whose key co-signs the Maker's side. If the ad
names a wallet other than the Maker itself (for example, an agent account
naming your own wallet), that wallet must approve the Maker first. This
stops anyone from naming your key on their ad without your consent. The
approval is on the **BLS Keys** page under **Maker approvals**.

## Retiring, replacing and revoking

- **Retire** ends a key now, on every registry. The relayer pays the gas.
  Retiring only shortens a key's life; it never moves funds.
- **Emergency stop** is a retirement you sign in advance. The **retirement
  service** stores it and submits it when it's needed: when you revoke an
  agent, or in an incident. A stored retirement can only end a key; it can
  never move funds.
- **Replace** a key by registering the next key index. In-flight trades
  keep their pinned key until they end.
- **Revoke** removes every key on the wallet. It's refused while the
  wallet has open trades.
